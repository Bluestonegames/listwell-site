Screening summary

This is a decision-support checklist, not an automatic accept/reject decision.

Important safeguards

Review fairly
  • Only run a credit check with the applicant’s permission; use an appropriate soft search through a legitimate referencing provider.
  • Assess affordability consistently. Benefits or a lack of conventional credit history should not automatically mean rejection.
  • Use consistent, non-discriminatory criteria and consider relevant explanations, alternative evidence or a guarantor.
  • Keep personal data secure, limit access and retention, and tell applicants how their information will be used.
  • Right to Rent is a separate England-only check and must be applied consistently to all adult prospective tenants.

Shelter: renting and credit checks · GOV.UK Right to Rent guidance

Prototype only: this does not connect to a credit bureau or referencing provider, verify documents, or make a legal eligibility decision. A production version needs secure storage, applicant consent records, privacy information and a compliant referencing-provider integration.

Official guidance & legal checks

Verify before use

This is intended for standard private residential assured periodic tenancies in England. It may not suit lodgers, licences, holiday lets, company lets, regulated tenancies, some student accommodation or properties outside England.

  • Give required written information about key tenancy terms before signing or otherwise agreeing the tenancy.
  • Check deposit protection, right-to-rent, gas and electrical safety, EPC, alarms, licensing and local requirements.
  • Do not add a fixed end date or section 21 clause; statutory rights override conflicting terms.
  • Use the current prescribed process for later rent increases or possession action; this agreement is not a notice.

Official written-information requirements · Renters’ Rights Act landlord guidance · Rent increase rules

This is a practical draft, not a solicitor-reviewed contract or guarantee of compliance. Seek qualified legal advice for unusual terms, HMOs and complex arrangements.
